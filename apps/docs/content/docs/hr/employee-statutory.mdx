## Employee statutory record

Each employee can have a one-to-one **statutory record** holding the tax and
payroll-affecting attributes that the country payroll rules need. Column names are
country-neutral — the country rule engine, not the schema, interprets them.

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Tax identification number | Statutory tax ID (Guyana: TIN). Sensitive — masked for non-payroll roles. |
| Social security number | Statutory social-security ID (Guyana: NIS). Sensitive — masked for non-payroll roles. |
| Dependent children | Drives the country rule's **child allowance**. |
| Has second job (+ pay) | Affects tax treatment of a second income. |
| Medical: on file / payroll deduction / external premium | Medical scheme attributes. |
| Other deductions | Miscellaneous recurring deduction amount. |

<Callout type="info" title="Who sees what">
  The tax and social-security identifiers are **masked for non-payroll roles**
  (only HR/payroll administrators see the full value), mirroring the bank
  account-number policy. Editing the statutory record is restricted to HR/payroll
  administrators, and every record is scoped to the caller's organization.
</Callout>

## No-login employees

Some staff exist for **HR and payroll purposes only** and never sign in — they have
no system account and no email on file. Heimdallone supports these as **no-login
employees**:

- An employee's email is **optional**. A no-login employee has no email and no
  linked user account.
- Email uniqueness still applies to employees that *do* have an email.
- In the employee profile, a missing email is shown as **"— (no login)"**.

<Callout type="info" title="Migration note">
  When live v1 data is migrated, employees without an email are imported as no-login
  employees (no fake placeholder address is invented). If such an employee should in
  fact have a login, an administrator adds a real email afterwards.
</Callout>
